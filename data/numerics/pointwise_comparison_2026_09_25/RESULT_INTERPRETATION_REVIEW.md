# Review of the computed continuous-loss results

Date: 25 September 2026. Interpretation of results supplied by the primary analysis; no additional test, refit or protocol change.

The envelope reduces equally galaxy-weighted mean outer squared standardized residual by 5.17 relative to the original core, with a pairs bootstrap-t interval of approximately [-8.45, -3.28]. The compact-control comparison has the same qualitative outcome. The recorded covariance sensitivities preserve the direction, and no single-galaxy omission reverses the mean. This supports a conditional improvement in the retained score, not identification of relative-field stress or a physical halo mechanism.

These averages have concentrated uncertainty: the largest core-comparison contribution supplies about 26% of the estimated variance and its variance-concentration count is about 7. The corresponding counts are about 4.7 for NFW and 3.9 for the calibrated baryon ablation. These are diagnostics, not replacement degrees of freedom. They mean that 131 galaxies do not contribute equally to the precision of the squared-loss mean. A lack of a single-galaxy direction reversal is reassuring about direction but does not establish stable tail probabilities or eliminate collective influence.

The NFW comparison should be reported as method-sensitive. Its observed mean loss is 10.53 lower than the envelope's. The primary wild-bootstrap p=.07146 does not reject the equal-mean-loss null at 5%. The marginal bootstrap-t interval for envelope-minus-NFW is [1.07, 27.67], and the percentile interval also narrowly excludes zero. These interval and test constructions are not inversions of one another. The interval resamples unrestricted empirical galaxy pairs and uses its asymmetric studentized distribution; the null-imposed Rademacher test uses a different reference distribution. They can disagree in finite samples, especially with skewness and concentrated variance. State both, and base any formal threshold language on the predeclared primary test. Do not claim a decisive statistical preference from the interval alone. Pooled-point uncertainty crossing zero and covariance sensitivities crossing zero for rho >= .5 further limit a universal superiority claim.

Recommended caption:

"Effects are envelope-minus-comparator mean loss; negative values favor the envelope. Intervals are marginal 95% whole-galaxy pairs bootstrap-t intervals. Probabilities use a distinct null-imposed wild-bootstrap procedure and the declared four-test Holm adjustment; the intervals are not simultaneous and do not invert that test."

For comparisons with zero wild-bootstrap exceedances among 99,999 draws, the reported raw plus-one estimate is 10^-5. With the recorded Holm adjustment it is 4 x 10^-5 and its Gaussian probability equivalent is about 4.11. Mark these cells as Monte Carlo resolution reached. This is not proof that the true tail is at or below 10^-5, and neither a lower bound on physical significance nor a measured detection at 4.11 sigma. Retain the recorded Monte Carlo tail-count uncertainty. The p-floor convention keeps an estimated tail nonzero but supplies no additional calibration.

Recommended table footnote:

"Monte Carlo resolution reached: zero of 99,999 replicates exceeded the observed statistic. The reported probability uses the plus-one convention; Z is only its Gaussian probability equivalent."

Do not describe the former fair-sign Z as having fallen to the new Z: the null hypotheses differ, and the new small-tail estimates are simulation-resolution-limited. A fair-sign test measures frequency of improvements; the continuous analysis measures their average magnitude. Differences between the two can be scientifically informative even if neither is a model-selection-adjusted discovery test.
